# Deliverable: workstream analysis

A workstream answers **one question** that the framing leaves open: how does a given journey unfold,
how to compute a given amount, which existing solution to reuse. The analysis gives findings, the
possible options and a reasoned recommendation, so that the PO decides and the specs start on a
checked basis.

- **Author**: one BA per workstream, several workstreams in parallel. **Contributor**: Architect,
  consulted on the feasibility of the options.
- **Phase**: P1. Goes through gate **G1** with the other specs deliverables. After G1, the PO may open
  a workstream to prepare a later phase or release (see below).
- **File**: `docs/10-analysis/<workstream>.html`, with `<workstream>` in kebab-case
  (`subscription-journey.html`, `price-calculation.html`).
- **Template**: [`template.html`](template.html), to copy then fill. Copying means reading
  `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/deliverable-analysis/template.html` with Read, then writing it to its
  place in the project with Write.
- **Inventory**: the page `docs/10-analysis/index.html` lists every analysis (author, progress,
  status, target (For), recommendation, what remains open, impacted deliverables). It is generated by
  `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/team/scripts/dashboard-pages.mjs` (skill `shipitright:deliverable-dashboard`)
  from the analysis pages: the BA does not write in it, they keep the header and the meta tags of
  their analysis up to date, which it reads.

## Splitting into workstreams (PO)

The PO splits P1 into **independent** workstreams: each has its question, its inputs, its file, and
can move forward without waiting for the others. Splits that work: by phase of the big picture, by
cluster of hotspots, by cross-cutting subject (calculation, complex rule, market benchmark). Two
workstreams that would need to read each other are in fact one workstream, or two workstreams in
sequence.

The brief of the BA gives: the name of the workstream, the question, its target (the phase or the
release it prepares, copied into the meta `target`), the inputs, the timebox (in number of passes or
in scope), what is outside the workstream.

## Workstream opened after G1

The PO may open a workstream after G1, to prepare a later phase or release. It follows the same
format and the same rules as in P1; its meta `target` carries the phase or release aimed at. The
stories that come out of it follow the rule of stories written after G1
(`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/PROCESS.md`, section 4, P2): the PO validates them (`AI ruling`, status
`validated`) and shows them to the human at the next G3; they go up to the human earlier if they
change the scope or carry a strong business rule.

## Inputs

- The brief of the workstream.
- `docs/00-framing/framing.html` and `docs/10-analysis/big-picture.html`.
- `.team/questions/`: questions and answers already known.
- The other analyses of `docs/10-analysis/` if the brief cites them (read only, we do not write in
  them).

## Target, progress, status and author

**Target**: the phase or the release the workstream prepares, field "For" of the header and meta
`target` (`P1`, `P2`, `P3`, or a release `R2`, `R3`…). A workstream of P1 carries `P1`. A workstream
opened after G1 carries the phase or release it aims at, given by the brief of the PO. The inventory
makes it its "For" column, and the dashboard page shows a "Workstream analyses" card in each column
of the Overview that has at least one workstream. In the header, a phase is written as text
(`<b>For</b> P2`) and a release in its tag, following the glossary rule on releases:
`<b>For</b> <span data-release>R2</span>`. For a target other than `P1`, the kicker of the header
becomes "Analysis for release R2" (or "Analysis for P2").

An analysis carries two distinct scales, each in the header and in a meta tag:

| Scale | Header | Meta | Values |
|---|---|---|---|
| Progress of the workstream | Workstream | `progress` | `in-progress` (in progress: the timebox is not used up, a pass remains), `done` (done), `partial` (partial delivery: timebox used up without a complete answer) |
| Status of the page | Status | `status` | `draft`, `in-review` (in review), `validated` (validated) |

Progress says whether the BA has finished, status whether the page has passed its review and its
gate. A done workstream can stay a draft; a partial workstream can be validated.

**Author**: "AI" (meta `author` set to `ai`) when a team agent handled the workstream, otherwise the
name of the person who did it (meta `author` set to that name). The BA instance does not appear in
the page: it stays traced by the journal (lines `dispatch` and `report`).

Tags to keep in the `<head>`, consistent with the Author, Status, For, Workstream and Date fields of
the header; their values are codes, never translated, and the date is in ISO:

```html
<meta name="type" content="analysis">
<meta name="author" content="ai">
<meta name="status" content="draft">
<meta name="progress" content="in-progress">
<meta name="target" content="P1">
<meta name="date" content="2026-10-02">
```

The Recommendation, Impacts on other deliverables and Questions and hypotheses sections carry
`data-section="recommendation"`, `"impacts"` and `"questions"`: the analyses inventory finds them by
this code, whatever the language of their title.

## Workstream too vague: stop and deliver

A workstream is handled like a spike: it is **timeboxed**. If the question cannot be answered within
the timebox (missing inputs, badly framed question, subject that breaks into several), the BA does not
force it. They stop and still deliver the analysis:

- progress `partial` in the header (meta `progress` set to `partial`) and in the section "Workstream
  question";
- the required sections filled with what is established, the rest as `Q-xxx` or as a hypothesis;
- in the recommendation: what is missing to conclude, and a proposed new split if the question is
  too broad.

An honest partial analysis is worth more than a recommendation based on inventions.

## Content

| Section | Level | What we expect |
|---|---|---|
| Header | required | the question of the workstream in the lede; author (AI or name), status of the page, target (For), progress of the workstream, aligned with the meta tags; every source read in the "Sources" line, as links |
| Workstream question | required | why it arises, what is outside the workstream, timebox and progress (in progress, done or partial) |
| Findings | required | what is established, each finding numbered `C1`, `C2` (anchor `id="C1"`) with its source as a link |
| Edge cases and exceptions | required | rare situations, errors, boundaries, with the expected behaviour or a `Q-xxx` |
| Solution exploration | recommended | leads considered, benchmark of what exists, as cards: principle, strengths, limits |
| Compared options | recommended | weighted criteria, score from 1 to 5 per option, total, recommended option highlighted. Descriptive workstream without a choice: write it |
| Recommendation | required | the option or conclusion chosen, why, under which conditions, what is needed to conclude if partial |
| Impacts on other deliverables | recommended | what the recommendation changes in the big picture, the story map, the rules, the UX, the design; each deliverable of the first column as a link, which the inventory takes over |
| Questions and hypotheses | required | `Q-xxx` asked to the PO, `HYPOTHESIS` taken to move forward. Empty list: write it |
| Cross-workstream synthesis | optional | links and tensions with the other workstreams, when the BA has read them |

No separate `options` deliverable: the comparison lives in each analysis, the synthesis across
workstreams in the optional section above or in the story map.

## Comparing the options

1. Choose 3 to 6 criteria drawn from the goals and constraints of the framing (value for the persona,
   cost of delivery, risk, compliance, lead time). Give them a weight from 1 to 3.
2. Score each option from 1 to 5 on each criterion (`score s1` to `s5`), with a short justification
   when the score is not obvious.
3. Total = sum of the scores multiplied by the weights. Highlight the recommended option
   (`tr.recommended`).
4. The recommendation may contradict the total: in that case, say so and explain why.

A technical feasibility criterion gets confirmed by the Architect (post in the forum, mention
`architect`). Without an answer, the score stays marked `HYPOTHESIS`.

## Quality rules

- **A finding cites its source**, as a link. Without a source, it is a hypothesis and is marked as
  such. The options, the recommendation and the impacts refer to the findings by their number
  (`<a href="#C2">C2</a>`).
- **The BA recommends, the PO decides.** The recommendation is reasoned; the business rule that
  follows from it is not settled until the PO has validated it.
- **Edge cases have a behaviour or a question**, never a blank.
- **Glossary terms are used word for word.**
- **We stay within the question of the workstream.** A neighbouring subject found along the way goes
  to a question or an impact, not to an extra analysis.

## Delivering

1. Copy the template to `docs/10-analysis/<workstream>.html`, replace `PROJECT_NAME` and
   `WORKSTREAM_NAME`, fill each section, delete the examples and the optional sections that do not
   apply. Keep the meta tags `author`, `status`, `target` and `progress` aligned with the header at
   each pass.
2. Create the questions `Q-xxx` in `.team/questions/` (field `blocking` filled).
3. Check: `node ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/team/scripts/check-deliverable.mjs docs/10-analysis/<workstream>.html`.
4. Report: path of the file, progress (in progress, done or partial) and status of the page,
   recommendation in one line, questions created, impacts to carry into the other deliverables. The
   PO then regenerates the inventory with the dashboard page.
