Security and permissions: who calls what

Session, roles, checks on the data. The backend checks, the front only hides. The principles are in the macro design; here, their application endpoint by endpoint.

Version v0.1 Date DD.MM.YYYY Author Architect Status draft

Session and access control

Purpose
Say how a request is authenticated, then authorized.
Content
  • The session mechanism.
  • The common check before each route.
  • The declaration of the roles per route.
  • The place of the checks on the data.
Why this formalism
The principles of the macro design applied to the code, as a short list, each with the invariant that makes it checkable.
  • session or token, cookie, duration, ADR as a link
  • common check before each route; public endpoints as links; without a session: 401
  • roles declared per route; insufficient role: 403

Rights matrix

Purpose
Know who can call what.
Content
In rows the endpoints, in columns the roles (yes or no), then the checks on the data beyond the role.
Why this formalism
The roles × operations access matrix, standard form of role-based access control (RBAC).
Reading
A public endpoint is also open to the roles.
Source
x-roles and x-data-control of openapi.yaml.

Data and protections

Purpose
Show what protects the data.
Content
  • Personal data and who sees it.
  • Passwords.
  • Tokens.
  • Injection.
  • Logs.
Why this formalism
One row per subject, with the invariant or the entity that carries it: the security review follows it as is.
  • Personal data: which, who sees it, what never goes out
  • Secrets and passwords: hash, rotation, or "none"
  • Injection: prepared statements, strict schemas
  • Logs: what is logged, without personal data in clear (INV-52)
