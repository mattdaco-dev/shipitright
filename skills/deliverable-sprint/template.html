Sprint goal

Purpose
Say in one sentence what the human will be able to do at the end of the sprint, so that the acceptance has an object and the review a criterion.
Content
A product role, what it does, on which screens. Set at planning, it no longer changes.
Why this format
A sentence checkable at acceptance: the human runs it or does not. The review says whether the goal is reached by referring to this sentence, not to the list of tickets.

Committed stories

Purpose
Fix the batch the PO chose and prove that each story can start.
Content
One row per story: epic, number of scenarios, story dependencies and their state, story DoR checked by the SM at planning.
Why this format
The number of scenarios is the one the campaign must turn green: it ties planning to the verdict. The DoR is the story's (its last section), not the tickets', which is checked further down.
Read
A story with an API contract operation still provisional at planning leaves the batch and has no row here. A story added or changed after G2 carries the mention "matrix updated" or does not start.

Tickets and waves

Purpose
Give the PO the list of tickets to create and the order to launch them, and prove that each one meets the DoR.
Content
The API contract version at planning, the operations of the batch and their state; one row per ticket: role, stories, what it produces and consumes, dependencies, contract, starting wave; the waves and the critical path; the shared files of each wave, with their decision.
Why this format
Tickets are cut from the pages of the detailed design (endpoints, processes, entities, screen view), not from the stories: the "produces, consumes" column cites those pages, and a process shared by two endpoints of the batch has a single producing ticket. Contract: frozen if the ticket produces or consumes API contract operations, all frozen; empty otherwise.
Read
A wave only holds tickets whose dependencies are closed. A front ticket on a frozen operation does not depend on the back ticket: it starts on a mock. An e2e ticket has no dependency (DoR: stories through G1, operations frozen, matrix up to date, brief that cites its scenarios and data sets); it starts in its wave and is tuned in Local as merges come in, so it may close after the following waves. Briefs are in the ticket files, not here. A shared file is a file several tickets of the same wave would change by hand (routes, registry, catalogue, exchange types): they would step on each other at merge. Each has its decision: generated by a script, split (one file per ticket, loaded by convention), or a single carrying ticket, cited in the brief of the others. A wave without a shared file: "none".

Review

Purpose
Say what the sprint delivered, what it did not deliver and why, and whether the sprint is done in the sense of its DoD.
Content
Delivered per story, with its acceptance result; not delivered, with its factual reason and where it restarts; the sprint DoD criterion by criterion, with its proof; the goal reached or not.
Why this format
The sprint DoD (PROCESS.md, section 7) is checked like the campaign exit criteria: met or not met, with the link that proves it. The review copies neither the campaign nor the acceptance report: it cites their verdict and points to them. The acceptance path run by the human stands in for a demo.
Read
A reservation RES-xx is defined in the acceptance report; here, only its reason and its follow-up. What is not delivered has a reason: blocked by what, split too big, review not converging; never "lack of time". A topic handled by the retro is not explained again: the review points to the problem Sn-Pm and the action Sn-Am that carry it.

Delivered

Retro

Purpose
Draw from the sprint what we keep and what is a problem, then turn each problem into a change checkable in the next sprint.
Content
A chain in four parts. Keep, with its sources. Problems, identified Sn-Pm (sprint number, then problem number: S2-P1…): finding, weight (the number of sources) and sources (Retro or Review section of a ticket, bug, forum topic, journal line). Actions, identified Sn-Am: what changes, the problem addressed cited by its Sn-Pm, the nature (one-sprint trial or lasting change), the owner (a role), the done criterion, the due point. Proposed plugin changes, identified Sn-Em: the file, as a path from the plugin root, the text before and after, the action Sn-Am that covers it, the human's decision. Identifiers are unique in the project and each is the anchor of its row.
Why this format
Consolidated by the SM from the Retro sections of the tickets, not from memory: a point without a source does not pass; seen in several tickets, a problem goes first. One source per topic: the finding, the weight and the sources are only written in the problem; the action cites Sn-Pm without copying them, the change cites Sn-Am without restating the reason, and the review, the metrics and the risks point to the identifiers. An action without an owner or a done criterion cannot be followed: "split better" does not pass.
Read
Each problem has at least one action. There is no separate "to try" list: a trial is an action of nature "one-sprint trial", judged at the next retro (kept, made lasting or abandoned). The done criterion is checked in the Follow-up of previous actions section of the next sprint. A change always targets a file of the ShipItRight plugin (PROCESS.md, agent file, convention, framework skill): it goes as a proposal to the framework repository, with the human's decision. A change specific to the project (project foundation, code organization, repository conventions) is an action, not a plugin change. The SM proposes, they do not modify: the change is submitted to the human by the PO, with the acceptance; until the decision, the linked action covers it.

Metrics

Purpose
Measure the sprint to compare sprints and adjust the roadmap.
Content
Tickets closed out of planned, average and maximum review rounds, bugs by severity and follow-up, blocked tickets and duration; estimated and actual time of the delivered stories, which the SM carries over into the roadmap calibration.
Why this format
Each number is recounted in the board (tickets' front matter) or the journal, never from memory. Estimated time comes from the roadmap estimate, actual time from the journal: their ratio corrects every story estimate (rules.calibration.sprints of the roadmap).
Read
Average review rounds cover the tickets of the plan; a fixed bug counts separately. The actual time of a story: the agent hours of its tickets, from launch to report, reviews and fixes included, read from the journal. A number is not explained here: if it comes from a retro topic, it points to its problem Sn-Pm or its action Sn-Am.

Follow-up of previous actions

Purpose
Check that the actions of the previous retro were done, before adding others.
Content
One row per action of sprint N-1, cited by its identifier Sn-Am as a link to the sprint N-1 page: owner, state (done, in progress, abandoned), the proof drawn from this sprint. An action whose problem comes back points to the Sn-Pm of this sprint.
Why this format
The done criterion of each action was set at the previous retro: we observe it, we do not debate it again. An abandoned action says why.
