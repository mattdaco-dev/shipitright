Accepted scope

Purpose
Say what the human accepted, on which version and where, so that the verdict is never read out of its context.
Content
The stories of the sprint, the branch and the accepted commit, the environment and its state, the date, the participants, the starting QA verdict.
Why this format
A definition list, one topic per term. The accepted commit is taken as is from the campaign, as a link to its runs: the human accepts the code the campaign played last, not another one.
Read
The QA verdict is cited, not copied: its criteria and its bugs are in the campaign. Acceptance only starts on a favorable verdict, with or without reservations, and on an environment reset by the QA.

Acceptance path

Purpose
Give the human a walkthrough they follow alone, on screen, and keep track of what they saw.
Content
15 minutes at most: steps numbered end to end, per journey, that target the decisions taken by delegation (PO rulings), the accepted limits and one complete journey; per step, scenario served, action with the data to enter, expected result, actual result, status.
Why this format
An acceptance test case: action, expected, actual. The expected result and the data come from the scenarios and data sets US-xxx/Dn of the stories, shifted to future dates without changing the day of the week; the actual result is noted during acceptance, with the words of the screen. Three statuses: ok, reservation (the gap becomes a reservation), ko (the human rejects the step).
Read
A journey, not one scenario per step: the human plays the whole path, the other scenarios and those the screen cannot trigger (technical error, past state) have no step, the end-of-journey line says where the campaign proves them. The order follows the recommendation of the QA verdict: the human first judges a reservation on screen.

Reservations

Purpose
List the gaps the human agrees to see fixed later, each with the ticket that fixes it.
Content
One row per reservation: identifier RES-xx, gap observed, story and step, ticket, due.
Why this format
A reservation without a ticket gets lost: the ticket exists in the board before signing. A minor bug left open at the QA verdict becomes a reservation, and its ticket is the existing BUG-xxx, left at status backlog.
Read
The RES-xx is stable: the sprint page, the roadmap and the next sprint cite it. A reservation is lifted when the item linked to it (the bug BUG-xxx or the ticket T-xxx created for it) moves to status done in the board; the signed report is not rewritten. No reservation: say so.

Accepted open bugs

Purpose
Keep track of a major bug delivered open, which only the human can accept.
Content
One row per open major: BUG-xxx, title, written acceptance by the PO in the campaign, reason given by the human, fix due.
Why this format
Open minors are reservations, they have no row here: a bug is recorded only once. An open blocking bug never gets here: the QA verdict would be unfavorable and acceptance would not take place.
Read
No open major: say so, and name the minors carried over as reservations.

Verdict and decision

Purpose
Record the human's decision and what it triggers.
Content
The verdict, the human's wording word for word, the merge decision, the decision maker, the date, the signature.
Why this format
Three verdicts: accepted, accepted with reservations (at least one reservation), rejected (the report says what is missing to accept). The verdict is the human's, neither the PO's nor the QA's: their wording is quoted.
Read
Accepted, with or without reservations: the human merges sprint/N into main, at the accepted commit. Rejected: the sprint stays open, nothing is merged.
Verdict: accepted / accepted with reservations / rejected "the human's wording"
