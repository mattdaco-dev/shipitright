ADR-NNNN: decision title, noun phrase

Foundation deviation

Purpose
State whether the decision deviates from the project's stack foundation. A deviation goes through an ADR with status proposed, which the PO brings to the human.
Content
No: what the foundation says on the subject and why the decision complies. Yes: the foundation rule or invariant set aside, and the scope of the deviation.
Why this format
One or two sentences: it is a fact checked at G2, not a debate; the debate is in "Rationale".

Context

Purpose
Say what forces a choice, so that a reader understands it with no other source three months from now.
Content
The problem, the constraints from framing, foundation and business rules, and the part of the question the ADR answers.
Why this format
A few sentences of prose, as in Michael Nygard's ADR format: context is told, not tabulated.

problem, constraints, what forces a choice; every cited identifier (BR-xx, Q-xxx, EP-xx…) as a link

Decision

Purpose
Say what is decided, applicable as is by a dev.
Content
One statement per choice, affirmative, with no condition or alternative.
Why this format
A list: each choice can be cited alone in a review or an invariant; the affirmative leaves no reading in doubt.
  • choice, affirmative

Rationale

Purpose
Say why this option, and why not the others.
Content
The arguments for the decision, then one row per rejected option with the reason for rejecting it.
Why this format
The considered options table of the MADR format: a rejected option and its reason stay visible, and the debate does not reopen without a new fact.
  • argument, tied to a constraint of the context

Consequences

Purpose
Say what the decision implies, good and bad, and what it requires doing.
Content
  • Positive: what it brings.
  • Negative: what it costs, the debt accepted.
  • Next steps: the actions to plan, with the deliverable or role that carries them.
Why this format
Three fixed terms: an ADR without a negative hides a cost, an ADR without next steps changed nothing.
